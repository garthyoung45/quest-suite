ONE is an approval gate for AI assistants. Any connected AI can ask for something through it. Nothing it asks for counts until you approve it with your fingerprint. Press STOP and every AI is shut out of ONE.

Try it now ↓ Watch it live

Unaudited · open for review

What it can't do: STOP only covers ONE. An AI that never goes through ONE isn't stopped by it. And an approval is carried out for one thing only: a test action that adds a line to one sandbox file.

How it works

One request, start to finish

Each step is tagged with what exists today. Anything that fails, times out or can't be checked is refused: deny by default.

  1. LiveAI asks

    A connected AI files a request with its own key. It gets back only a receipt number.

  2. ProposedPolicy check

    Today every request goes straight to the owner. No automatic rule check sorts them yet.

  3. LiveOwner decides

    Approve needs the owner's passkey, for that exact request, once. Deny and STOP are always available.

  4. LiveSealed record

    The decision is written to a hash-chained record, copied hourly to a home computer.

  5. Self-reported60-second wait

    The runner waits 60 seconds before acting, so STOP can still catch it.

  6. Self-reportedRunner acts

    One allowlisted test action only: add a line to one sandbox file. First run 17 Nov.

  7. ProposedProof of what happened

    The runner reports "done". A signed proof of the result, checkable by others, is not built.

STOP pressed every waiting request is cancelled; AI calls are refused; lifting it needs the passkey.
Owner denies or ignores denied requests are recorded; unanswered ones expire after 7 days.
Something breaks if ONE can't read STOP or write its record, it refuses everything (offline-tested).
Bad approval wrong key, wrong request, changed wording, reused or expired: refused (try it in the lab).

Trust boundaries: the AI apps are outside ONE and untrusted. The owner's phone holds the passkey. ONE's server and the record are run by me, so they are not independent. Whoever controls the hosting account could change ONE's code.

    06 · Governance Center
    
  
    Three rules. That's the whole idea.
    
  
    
    
    AIs ask
    A connected AI can read the status and, with the right key, file a request. That is all it can do. It cannot approve, change rules, or touch anything.
    
    
    You approve
    Only your passkey says yes. Each approval is for one exact request, works once, and is written into a sealed record.
    
    
    STOP wins
    One button freezes everything new in ONE and cancels every waiting request. Undoing it needs your passkey. It only covers ONE: an AI working outside ONE is not stopped by it.

    Words used on this page, in plain English
    
    
    
      
    AI assistant
    A chatbot like ChatGPT or Claude that can be connected to other tools.
    
      
    Request
    Something an AI asks to do. On its own it does nothing.
    
      
    Passkey
    A sign-in that lives on the owner's phone and needs their fingerprint. It cannot be typed or guessed.
    
      
    Approval
    The owner's passkey signature for one exact request. It works once and then runs out.
    
      
    STOP
    One button that refuses every AI and cancels everything waiting. Only the passkey lifts it. It only covers ONE.
    
      
    Runner
    A small program that carries out an approved request. Today it can do one test action only.
    
      
    Record / ledger
    A list of everything that happened. Each line includes a fingerprint (hash) of the line before, so a quiet edit shows up.
    
      
    Hash
    A short code computed from some text. Change one letter and the code changes completely.
    
      
    Fail closed
    When something breaks, the safe choice is to refuse, not to let things through.
    
      
    Self-reported
    It comes from my own logs. Nobody else has checked it.
    
      
    Independently verified
    Checked by someone outside, with evidence you can trace to them. Nothing here is, yet.
    
    
    
  
    

  
    How one request travels, step by step
    
    
      
      
    1. An AI app (Claude, ChatGPT and others) is connected to ONE through a standard connector (MCP) with its own key.
      2. 
      
    2. The AI calls get_rules and reads the owner's standing rule: nothing consequential without the owner's explicit word.
      3. 
      
    3. The AI calls send_request with a title and details. ONE stores it as waiting and returns only an id. Limits: 3 waiting and 5 a day per key; each expires after 7 days.
      4. 
      
    4. The owner sees it in the owner app, behind a sign-in wall. Approve opens the phone's passkey prompt; the signature is bound to that one request and works once.
      5. 
      
    5. Approved: the request becomes a Mission on the owner's list. If it is the one allowlisted test action, a runner carries it out after a 60-second wait (STOP halts it). Otherwise nothing else happens.
      6. 
      
    6. The AI can call list_requests to see what became of its own requests, and nobody else's.
      7. 
      
    7. STOP: every waiting request is cancelled and every AI call is refused until the owner resumes with a passkey. A resume signed before a STOP cannot undo that STOP.
      8. 
      
    8. Every step is written to a hash-chained record, copied hourly to a home computer.
      9.

    How it was attacked
    
    
    Offline, against a copy of the live build with the network blocked: 70 scripted attack cases, 63 held, 7 are limits I wrote down. In those tests STOP refused 736 writes in a row, approvals could not be replayed or moved to a different request, and it failed closed in 7 error cases.
    
    
    All of it was designed, run and graded by AI sessions on my account. That is the weak point. A human outsider rerunning it is the first thing I am asking for.
    
  
    

  
    The six-AI experiment (a negative result)
    
    
    Separately, I tested whether six AI models reviewing together beat one. Two made-up maintenance documents with planted errors and answer keys fixed (hashed) before any run.
    
    
    
      
    Best single modelSix as a panel
    
      
    Lockout procedure, 10 defects10 of 109 of 10 (both runs)
    
      
    Arithmetic summary, 7 errors7 of 77 of 7 (both runs)
    
      
    Cost of a correct answerunder 1 cent12 to 13 cents
    
    
    
    
    The panel never beat the best single model, cost 21 to 85 times more, but did fix weaker models' answers on the arithmetic task. Small test, keys written by one of the tested models, so it proves nothing general. Results · raw runs and the script to rebuild the table.

    Who built it and why
    
    
    I'm Garth Young, an industrial maintenance technician in New Hampshire. My trade runs on lockout: before anyone touches a machine, the power is locked off and only the person holding the lock can turn it back on. AI assistants are getting hands. I wanted the same lock for them, on my own phone.
    
    
    I built ONE over about a month of evenings, with AI coding assistants doing most of the typing and me deciding what it may and may not do.
